Medicine expiry tracking and FEFO: a step-by-step guide

Expiry control reduces the risk of selling unsuitable stock and helps teams identify products that need review. The important details are batch-level dates, careful dispensing and repeatable routines.

Updated October 2026

Guides › Medicine expiry tracking and FEFO: a step-by-step guide

1. Record the relevant expiry information

Where applicable, record batch or lot identifiers and the printed expiration date when receiving medicines. Inspect labels and packaging; do not invent missing dates or reuse expiry information from a different batch.

2. Apply FEFO where appropriate

First-expiring, first-out (FEFO) means reviewing the shortest remaining shelf life first, while respecting storage conditions and the requirements for that medicine. Staff must not dispense expired products.

3. Review and segregate risk stock

Use routine reports or physical checks to find near-expiry and expired items. Segregate unsuitable products and document approved returns, quarantine or disposal according to applicable requirements.

FAQ

What is FEFO in a pharmacy?

FEFO is a stock handling method that gives priority to products with the earliest applicable expiration date.

Is a low-stock report the same as an expiry report?

No. Stock quantity and remaining shelf life are different controls, and pharmacy teams should review both.
